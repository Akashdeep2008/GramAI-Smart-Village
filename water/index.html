SMALL STEPS, SHARED IMPACT

Where can we save water?

Choose an area to explore practical checks and first actions. Local systems and climate differ, so adapt these ideas to your panchayat or water utility.

⌂AT HOME

Find and fix leaks

Check taps, flushes, storage tanks and visible pipe joints. Repair a leak promptly and avoid letting taps run while washing.

  • Read the meter or note tank level at a quiet time.
  • Use a bucket where practical; reuse suitable rinse water for non-food plants.
  • Keep drinking-water storage covered and clean.
☂RAINWATER

Catch seasonal rain

Keep roof gutters and downpipes clear. A suitable system can direct roof runoff to storage or recharge, depending on site conditions.

  • Use a first-flush arrangement and suitable filter.
  • Inspect the tank, screen and overflow before the rains.
  • Get recharge structures checked for local soil, groundwater and contamination risks.
♧ON THE FARM

Match irrigation to need

Check moisture in the crop root zone and the weather before irrigating. Where suitable, drip, mulch, field levelling and repaired channels can reduce avoidable losses.

  • Do not use a one-size-fits-all watering timetable.
  • Keep field drains working after heavy rain.
  • Ask local agriculture staff which water-saving method fits the crop and soil.
◉COMMUNITY

Protect local sources

Keep waste, sewage, animal waste and chemicals away from wells, ponds, streams and hand pumps. Report damage or suspected contamination to the responsible local authority.

  • Keep source surroundings clean and avoid direct dumping.
  • Do not enter tanks, wells or confined spaces to clean them.
  • Record the location, date and visible issue for the panchayat or utility.
A ROUGH PLANNING ESTIMATE

How much rain might your roof collect?

Use roof catchment area, seasonal rainfall and an estimated runoff factor. It is an upper planning estimate, not a promise of usable water or a recharge design.

Estimated litres = roof area (m²) × rainfall (mm) × runoff factor

Enter your local figures to estimate seasonal roof runoff.Actual collection is lower due to first flush, leakage, evaporation, overflow and system condition. Do not use harvested water for drinking without appropriate treatment and testing.
WATER QUALITY MATTERS

Clear water does not always mean safe water.

Appearance, smell and taste cannot confirm that water is safe to drink. If water quality is in doubt, follow your local public-health or water-supply authority’s instructions and use an authorized testing laboratory.

If you notice a sudden change

Stop using the affected source for drinking until the responsible authority advises you. Report the source and location to your panchayat, local water utility or public-health office. Do not guess at chemical treatment.
